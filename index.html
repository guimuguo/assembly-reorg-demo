Assembly Reorganization Explorer

A rat learns that two cues predict two foods. Then one food is made aversive. Do the prefrontal neurons that fired together for that cue regroup? This prototype shows how the planned pipeline turns each recording session into a network, finds the cell assemblies inside it, and scores how well they reorganize. Every number on this page is simulated to illustrate the workflow; no recordings have been analyzed yet.

    Aim 2: does reorganization predict behavior?

    Each point is one simulated rat (17 wild-type, 17 TgF344-AD, as in the published cohort). The devaluation index measures how well the rat stopped responding to the devalued cue. In the published single-neuron analysis, cue responsiveness predicted behavior in wild-type rats but not in AD rats. The design goal of the network measure is to predict behavior in both genotypes.

    How the pipeline works

    1 · Data
    Sorted spike times
    34 rats, 10 conditioning sessions and a post-devaluation test; task events time-stamped. Converted to Neurodata Without Borders.
    2 · Networks
    One graph per session and epoch
    Units are nodes; an edge means co-firing above a null from 1,000 spike-jittered surrogates. More than 1,800 small graphs.
    3 · Assemblies
    Dense subgroups, certified
    k-truss cores, communities, quasi-cliques, and recurring patterns; kept only if absent from surrogates (FDR 5%).
    4 · Tracking
    Match across epochs and days
    Membership overlap links assemblies through learning and across the devaluation.
    5 · Metrics
    Four numbers per animal
    Formation, cue–outcome binding, reorganization index, fragmentation index.
    6 · Validation
    Against behavior
    Mixed models, leave-one-animal-out prediction, and a graph neural network trained on the graph library.

    Why this matters

    Loss of behavioral flexibility is among the earliest signs of Alzheimer's disease, yet no measure of it exists at the level of brain circuits. The reorganization index is designed to be that measure: a single number per animal that can be compared between genotypes, correlated with behavior, and, in later work, tested as a target for intervention.
